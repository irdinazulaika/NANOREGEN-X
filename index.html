The Spark ML Model

What published data can and cannot tell us.

We built a systematic dataset of electrospun PCL nerve scaffolds and tested eight learners with study-level cross-validation. The answer was mostly no. That result shapes the tool.

181studies included, 889 scaffold arms, every value with a verbatim quote
0 of 32learner and target pairs beat a mean-only model across studies (Holm-adjusted)
τ 0.34 to 0.56within-study ranking of formulations above chance for contact angle and conductivity

What works

Ranking within one labContact angle: 59 held-out studies, all eight learners, mean Kendall τb 0.34 to 0.47. Conductivity: 16 studies, τb 0.42 to 0.56. Adjusted p ≤ 0.004.
External testContact angle of 34 unseen studies: ridge 13% lower RMSE (95% CI 5 to 20), GPR 14% (3 to 24), MT-DE 10% (6 to 15).
Design leversSHAP on XGBoost: PCL fraction (28%) and a protein or bioactive coating (13%) drive contact angle; filler loading and type drive conductivity.

What does not

Absolute prediction for a new labBest case 10% error reduction (random forest, contact angle), adjusted p = 0.115. Study effects explain most of the variance.
Modulus and strengthNo learner beat the mean, in cross-validation or on new studies. Dropped from the tool.
One-line rules"Plain PCL is the most hydrophobic" and "more filler, more conductive" rank as well as the learners. We say so.

So the tool does three things

1. Curated database

181 studies, 2,551 extracted values, PRISMA search of PubMed and Scopus, second reading of a sample, duplicates removed. Published with the paper.

2. Rank, not forecast

Enter your candidate formulations. The model orders them relative to your own control. It does not promise a number for your lab.

3. Grows with your data

Fill the Excel template with your own measurements. The platform merges them with the literature table and refits, so the model learns your laboratory's offset.

Where the numbers come from

Our runs22 Oct, 15% w/v PCL in DCM, 20 kV, 10 cm, 1 mL/h, 18G, 1500 rpm: median 359 nm (IQR 261 to 477, about 1,700 fibres), 63 to 84% within ±10°, bead-free. 8 Oct graphene mats: 152 to 245 nm median. 20% PCL: 5 to 12 µm ribbons.
Literature trendNeat PCL, 51 arms from 28 studies: log10 diameter rises 0.031 per % w/v (about 2.3× from 8 to 20%). Direction agrees with Deitzel et al. 2001; it has no out-of-study skill, so it only scales our own anchor.
RulesBeads when viscosity is too low (Fong 1999): flagged below 10% or above 2 mL/h. Surface pores above about 45% RH (Casper 2004; Nezarati 2013). Alignment rises with drum speed (Matthews 2002); our 1500 rpm gives the measured 63 to 84%.

Composition is read from the dope (graphene) and the post-spin step (peptide); the fibre properties shown reuse the side A model. Side B is a design window, not a validated predictor. Voltage, distance and flow were not extracted in the literature study; their effects here follow the cited mechanisms and are scaled to our own two runs. Every new run we spin is added to the Excel template and tightens these windows.

Conductivity: our own measurement beats any literature model

Four-point probe, 15 wt% PCL mats

Percolation between 5 and 10 wt% graphene. 15 wt% reaches 0.32 S/cm. Mean ± SD, n = 5 to 6.

Why we trust it more

Literature124 arms from 30 studies span 11 orders of magnitude; no learner beats the mean; our 0.2 to 0.6 wt% range sits below percolation in every reported system.
Our matsSame polymer, same method, same instrument. 0.2 to 2 wt%: below detection. 5 wt%: 2.4 × 10-5. 10 wt%: 0.022 ± 0.006. 15 wt%: 0.32 ± 0.07. 20 wt%: 0.43 ± 0.03 S/cm.
Decision15 wt% graphene is the working loading. Raman confirms multilayer platelets (ID/IG 0.94); DSC shows crystallinity rising from 39% to 54% with loading.
